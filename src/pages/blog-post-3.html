How to clean and condition leather seats without ruining them

A ten-minute routine every few months keeps leather soft and stops it from cracking in the Coimbatore sun. Here are the products to use and the ones to avoid.

Rohan MenonLeather and finishes lead · 14 Sep 2026 · 5 min read

Leather is skin. It dries out, it stains and it fades if it is ignored, but it lasts for well over a decade with a little attention. The most common damage we repair is not wear: it is cracking from heat and harsh cleaners.

What you need

  • Two soft microfibre cloths
  • A pH-neutral leather cleaner
  • A water-based leather conditioner (no silicone)
  • A soft upholstery brush for the seams
  • A vacuum with a brush attachment

The routine, step by step

  1. Vacuum the seats, using the brush along the stitching where grit collects.
  2. Spray cleaner onto the cloth, not the seat, and wipe one panel at a time.
  3. Work the brush gently into seams and perforations, then wipe dry straight away.
  4. Apply a thin layer of conditioner with a fresh cloth and let it soak in for 15 minutes.
  5. Buff off the excess. The seat should feel soft, not greasy or shiny.

What to avoid

  • Dish soap, wet wipes and all-purpose cleaners: they strip the oils from the leather.
  • Silicone shine sprays: slippery, dust-attracting and hard to remove.
  • Soaking perforated leather: water gets into the foam and smells for weeks.

How often?

Wipe down monthly and condition every three months. In summer, or if the car lives outside, condition every two months and use a windscreen sunshade.

Studio tip

Leatherette covers need none of this. A damp cloth with a drop of mild soap is enough. Save the conditioner for real leather.
