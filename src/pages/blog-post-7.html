Monsoon car interior care: keeping damp, mould and smells away

When the rains arrive, wet shoes and closed windows turn the cabin humid. A few habits keep seats, mats and carpets fresh until the dry season.

Suresh BalajiWorkshop manager · 18 Jul 2026 · 5 min read

The monsoon is hard on interiors. Water comes in on shoes, umbrellas and wet clothes, and with the windows closed it has nowhere to go. Within a couple of weeks you get a musty smell and, in the worst cases, mould under the carpet.

Daily habits

  • Swap carpet mats for rubber trays for the season.
  • Keep a dry microfibre towel in the door pocket for seats and hands.
  • Store wet umbrellas in a bag or a boot tray, not on the floor.
  • Run the air conditioning for a few minutes before parking to dry the air.

Weekly checks

  1. Lift the mats and press the carpet with a dry cloth to feel for damp.
  2. Leave the windows open a crack in covered parking on dry afternoons.
  3. Wipe leather and leatherette seats so water marks do not set.
  4. Check door rubber seals and the sunroof drains for blockages.

If the carpet gets soaked

Act fast. Remove the mats, soak up as much water as possible with towels, and run the heater with the windows slightly open. If the underlay stays wet for more than two days, it needs to be lifted and dried properly, which we do at the studio.

Getting rid of smells

Air fresheners only cover a damp smell. Find the source, dry it, and then leave an open box of baking soda or a bag of activated charcoal under a seat for a week.

Studio tip

Fabric seat covers can be removed and washed after the season. Ask us for a care card and we will show you how to take them off and refit them without stretching.
