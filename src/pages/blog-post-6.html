Covers for 7-seaters: what Innova, Ertiga and XUV700 families should know

Three rows, folding seats, child seats and snacks. A 7-seater cabin works harder than any other, and the covers need to keep up.

Ananya KrishnanCustomer design consultant · 02 Aug 2026 · 6 min read

Seven-seat cars are family cars. They carry grandparents on Sunday and school bags on Monday, and the second and third rows fold and slide constantly. Covers that bunch up or block levers are the most common complaint we hear about cheap sets.

Cut-outs for every lever and latch

Each 7-seater has its own folding mechanism. Our patterns leave exact openings for the tumble levers, recline straps and ISOFIX points, so nothing needs to be lifted or tucked away to fold a seat.

Child seats and ISOFIX

  • Keep ISOFIX anchors fully accessible through reinforced slots.
  • Choose a material that does not dent under a child seat base: leatherette or fabric.
  • Ask for a thin protector panel on the second-row seat back for little feet.

Materials that survive families

Leatherette is the clear favourite: juice, crumbs and muddy shoes wipe off. For the third row, which gets less use and more heat from the rear glass, fabric or mesh keeps it cooler.

Fitting time

A full 7-seater set takes us up to six hours at the studio, against three to four for a 5-seater. Book a morning slot, or use our free pick-up and drop within city limits.

Studio tip

Mixing materials is fine. Many families choose leatherette for rows one and two and breathable fabric for row three, with matching thread so the cabin still looks like one design.
